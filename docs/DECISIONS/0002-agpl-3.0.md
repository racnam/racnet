# ADR-0002: AGPL-3.0 license

**Status:** accepted · **Date:** 2026-07-29

## Context

bitchat's history includes "Noghteha", a closed-source fork mass-distributed
in Iran (72k+ downloads in three days) to users with no way to verify what
they had sideloaded. For software in this category — sideloaded during
internet shutdowns by people at real risk — unverifiable closed forks are a
concrete harm vector, not a hypothetical. The license is the only lever the
project has over downstream forks.

## Decision

License the entire repository under AGPL-3.0.

## Consequences

- Anyone distributing a modified client or running a modified anchor node as
  a network service must publish their source; closed forks of the kind
  Noghteha represents would violate the license.
- The license cannot *prevent* bad-faith closed forks — enforcement is civil
  and jurisdiction-bound — but it denies them legitimacy and app-store cover.
- Copyleft may deter some commercial embedders and contributors. Accepted:
  adoption of the *protocol* is unaffected (specs aren't code; independent
  implementations under any license remain possible and are an explicit
  project goal), and the substrate-not-app differentiation lives in the spec.
- Relicensing later requires consent of all copyright holders; while the
  project is solo this remains cheap, another reason to decide now.

## Alternatives rejected

- **Apache-2.0 / MIT:** maximizes adoption but permits exactly the closed-fork
  scenario that motivated this decision.
- **MPL-2.0:** file-level copyleft still allows shipping the core inside a
  closed app wholesale.
- **GPL-3.0:** nearly equivalent for distributed apps, but leaves the anchor
  node's network-service use uncovered; AGPL closes that gap at no extra cost.
